# Bank-of-England-Rate-Analysis
**Time Series Event Analysis: Using frequency, volatility, and % change to explain What Moves the Bank Rate from 17% to 0.10%  (1975-2021)**

# Business Problem
**Rate changes affect mortgages, loans, savings, and business borrowing for the whole UK. The Bank of England needs to understand what influences the Bank Rate, so it can prepare for future changes**

# Business Questions
**What are the main events that decide Bank Rate changes?
  How much can these changes affect the Bank Rate? (% Fall or % Rise)
  How volatile can each rate get within a set period?**

# Key Findings 
*1976 Sterling Crisis and IMF Loan.
1990–1993 Black Wednesday and ERM exit.
2008–2009 Global Financial Crisis.
2016 Brexit referendum.
COVID-19 pandemic*

**The 1976 Sterling Crisis and IMF Loan (1976–1982)- The British Economy went into heavy debt,borrowed £2.3bn from IMF, pounds crashed in 1976 and the effect lingered for 7 years between 1976-1982, this series of event including Falklands War forced the Bank to intervene repeatedly to defend sterling set the Bank rate high and volatile to external influence((IMF policy- Spending Cut, high interest, Zero money printing). The Bank was forced to keep rates high and react constantly, from huge panic drop in November 1977 Rate was 5.00 to huge Rate rise of 17.00 in November 1979 and many small steering moves in 1982 (36 changes)**

# 1990-1993 
**The Black Wednesday (Fall from 13.88% to 5.75%)- The UK joined the ERM in 1990(at the time the rate was completely stable) and had to keep rates high to defend the pound, even during a recession. On Black Wednesday (16 Sept 1992), the pound collapsed and Britain was forced out of the ERM. Free from the peg, the Bank cut rates sharply from 13.88% to 5.75%. This was the second major regime change after the 7 Years crisis.**

# 2008- 2009
**Global Financial Crisis- Lehman collapsed in Sept 2008, freezing the global banking system, the effect was huge on the UK Economy, British Bank like RbSm Lloyds and Bank of england was exposed forcing them to cut rate no save the economy. The Bank of England cut rates from 5.75% to 2.0% by end of 2008, then to 0.50% in March 2009 to stop the economy from collapsing. The rate then stayed flat at 0.50% until 2016**

# 2016
**Brexit Referendum- The rate had been flat at 0.50% for 7 years since 2019. After the Leave vote in June 2016, the Bank cut it to 0.25% in August 2016. As the economy held up, it raised it back to 0.50% in 2017 and 0.75% in 2018.**

# 2020
**COVID- Rate has been at 0.75% prior to Covid, lockdown hit in March 2020 and the Bank cut rates twice in 8 days, from 0.25% to 0.10%, the lowest ever. The rate then stayed flat at 0.10% through 2021 and rose back to 0.25% at the end of December 2021**

# Recommendations-
**Monitor economic indicators: Track inflation, GDP, unemployment rate and consumer spending power to identify economic pressures that could influence Bank Rate decisions**
**Monitor banking-sector stability: Track bank liquidity, lending and credit availability. This flags financial crisis risk early.**
**Prepare for economic shocks: Develop contingency scenarios for extreme events(1979, 2008 and 2020 ) such as wars, pandemics, political instability and financial crises, using historical rate movements to assess potential impacts on Bank Rate.**
**Build predictive models: Combine historical Bank Rate data with economic and banking indicators to improve forecasting.**
